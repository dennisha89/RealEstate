## 1. INSTITUTIONAL REAL ESTATE INVESTMENT SIGNALS

### Data Sources
- **NCREIF ODCE Index**: Benchmark of core institutional real estate funds; tracks quarterly total returns. Several consecutive quarters of positive returns signal market recovery.
- **Nareit (reit.com)**: Tracks IPOs, secondary equity/debt offerings, capital raised for publicly-listed U.S. REIT securities.
- **McKinsey Global Private Markets Report**: Annual data on deal value, transaction counts, strategy-level returns. 2025 deal value reached $842B (up 10% YoY).
- **JLL Global Real Estate Perspectives**: Quarterly global transaction volumes; Q4 2025 volumes were 15% higher YoY. Full-year 2025 saw 19% increase from 2024.
- **CBRE Investment Management**: Institutional capital flow reports, sector allocation data.
- **Apollo, Blackstone, Morgan Stanley**: Published annual outlooks with capital deployment data.

### Accessibility
- **NCREIF**: Paid subscription (institutional); quarterly data
- **Nareit**: Free public data portal at reit.com/data-research
- **McKinsey/JLL/CBRE**: Published reports (some free, some gated)
- **SEC filings**: Free (EDGAR) for public REITs and fund disclosures

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Transaction volume increases signal capital flowing into real estate markets
- Cap rate compression expectations (71% of investors expect it in 2026) signal anticipated price increases
- Sector rotation data shows WHERE institutional money is flowing (away from office, into logistics, data centers, SFR)
- Fundraising momentum and decreasing redemption queues signal renewed institutional appetite

### Who Uses It
- Pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, endowments
- REIT portfolio managers for capital allocation
- Real estate PE firms for timing deployment
- Commercial brokers for market timing

## 2. FOREIGN REAL ESTATE INVESTMENT (CFIUS/FIRPTA)

### Data Sources
- **NAR International Transactions Report**: Annual survey tracking foreign buyer purchases. 2025 data: $56B in purchases (78,100 properties), up 33.2% YoY. Top origins: China (15%), Canada (14%), Mexico (8%), India (6%), UK (4%). Top destinations: FL (21%), CA (15%), TX (10%).
- **CFIUS Annual Reports**: Published by Treasury. 2023: 342 filings reviewed (109 declarations, 233 notices). 3 declarations involved real estate.
- **FIRPTA Withholding Data**: IRS tracks 15% withholding on foreign dispositions of US real property interests.
- **USDA Foreign Agricultural Land Portal**: Foreign entities must report ownership of U.S. agricultural land (Agricultural Foreign Investment Disclosure Act).
- **Foreign Investment Watch (foreigninvestmentwatch.com)**: Tracks CFIUS disclosures, mitigation agreements, foreign transactions via spreadsheets.

### Accessibility
- **NAR Report**: Published annually (free summary, full report behind NAR membership)
- **CFIUS Annual Reports**: Free via Treasury.gov
- **FIRPTA data**: Not publicly available at granular level; aggregate data via IRS statistics
- **USDA portal**: Public reporting portal
- **Foreign Investment Watch**: Subscription service

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Foreign buyer volume and origin-country shifts reveal international capital flows into specific U.S. markets
- 47% of international buyers pay all-cash, indicating pure capital inflow
- CFIUS filing volume signals foreign interest in U.S. commercial/strategic real estate
- Destination state data (FL, CA, TX dominate) shows geographic concentration

### Who Uses It
- NAR members and residential brokers in gateway markets
- National security analysts (CFIUS)
- Luxury real estate developers (foreign buyer preferences)
- Policy researchers studying capital flows

## 3. 1031 EXCHANGE DATA

### Data Sources
- **IRS Form 8824**: Filed by taxpayers completing like-kind exchanges. The IRS does NOT publish aggregate 1031 exchange volumes publicly.
- **IPX1031 (Nation's Largest Intermediary)**: Industry trend reports based on their transaction volume. Published at ipx1031.com.
- **Federation of Exchange Accommodators (FEA)**: Trade organization with member data.
- **Qualified Intermediary (QI) industry reports**: Companies like IPX1031, Asset Preservation Inc., and others publish volume trends.

### Accessibility
- **IRS aggregate data**: NOT publicly available by market. Would require FOIA or academic data-sharing agreement.
- **QI industry reports**: Free (published trend summaries), proprietary (detailed data)
- **Individual Form 8824 data**: Private tax return information

### How It Signals Money Movement
- 1031 exchange volume indicates investor capital recycling within real estate (selling one property, buying another)
- Sector shifts visible in exchange patterns: investors moving FROM office TO industrial/multifamily
- Geographic migration: investors exchanging from high-cost to lower-cost markets
- Volume drops signal capital "freezing" in place; volume increases signal active redeployment
- Maturing CRE loans (significant volume coming due) are expected to drive 1031 activity as owners sell and re-deploy

### Who Uses It
- Real estate investors and their tax advisors
- QI companies for market positioning
- CRE brokers to anticipate buyer demand
- Policy researchers (1031 has been a perennial target for tax reform)

## 4. HMDA MORTGAGE DATA

### Data Sources
- **CFPB HMDA Data Portal (consumerfinance.gov/data-research/hmda/)**: The most comprehensive public database of U.S. mortgage market activity. Near-universe coverage: 90%+ in metro counties, ~80% in remote non-metro counties. 99 fields per record.
- **FFIEC HMDA Data Browser (ffiec.cfpb.gov/data-browser/)**: Interactive query tool for HMDA data.
- **Urban Institute HMDA Interactive Map**: Mortgage originations by census tract, broken down by race/ethnicity over 16 years.
- **Philadelphia Fed Home Mortgage Explorer**: Lending statistics for first-lien mortgages, 2010-2023.
- **University of Michigan NANDA/HLD**: HMDA data aggregated by census tract.

### Accessibility
- **FULLY PUBLIC AND FREE**. Bulk downloads, APIs, interactive browsers all available.
- Data released annually; covers virtually all U.S. mortgage lending.

### How It Signals Money Movement
- **Loan origination volume by geography**: Shows where mortgage capital is flowing
- **Investment property flag**: HMDA distinguishes primary residence, second home, and investment property loans. Only 325,850 applications were for second homes/investment properties in 2024.
- **Denial rates by area**: High denial rates may signal credit tightening or risk perception
- **Loan amounts and property values**: Track capital intensity by census tract
- **Lender activity**: Which lenders are most active in which markets
- **Second home/investment property share**: Dropped from 5.29% (2021) to 2.85% (2024) -- a direct measure of investor retreat

### Who Uses It
- Federal regulators (fair lending enforcement)
- Academic researchers (housing finance, discrimination studies)
- Real estate investors and analysts (market heat maps)
- PropTech companies building lending analytics products
- Community development organizations

## 5. LLC FORMATION TRACKING

### Data Sources
- **U.S. Census Bureau Business Formation Statistics (BFS)**: Monthly data on EIN applications, classified by NAICS industry codes (including real estate). Covers high-propensity business applications (HBA) and applications with planned wages (WBA). Released ~11-12 days after month end. Available at census.gov/econ/bfs/.
- **State Secretary of State Filings**: Each state's business filing division records Articles of Organization for LLCs. Varying accessibility.
- **Registered Agents Inc. Business Formation Report**: Third-party tracking of state-level LLC formations. January 2026 showed strong formation numbers level with January 2025.

### Accessibility
- **Census BFS**: Free, public, monthly, state-level, NAICS-coded. Downloadable CSV/API.
- **State SOS data**: Varies by state. Some states provide bulk download or search APIs; others require manual lookup or FOIA.
- **Third-party reports**: Free summaries, paid detailed data.

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Spikes in real estate NAICS-coded EIN applications signal new investment entity formation
- State-level patterns reveal where investors are setting up shop (e.g., NC, WV, KY saw 48-55% MoM gains in Jan 2026)
- Series LLC formations indicate portfolio-scale investment activity
- Delaware/Wyoming/Nevada formations may indicate out-of-state investor activity (privacy/tax benefits)
- LLC formation is a LEADING indicator -- entities form before acquisitions

### Who Uses It
- Economic development agencies
- Academic researchers studying entrepreneurship
- Real estate market analysts (under-explored signal)
- PropTech companies building alternative data products

## 6. SBA LOAN DATA

### Data Sources
- **SBA Open Data Portal (data.sba.gov)**: All SBA 7(a) and 504 loan program data, segmented by decade. Includes data dictionaries.
- **SBA Lender Reports (sba.gov/partners/lenders/lender-reports)**: Summarizes approvals by state and lender.
- **SBA Disaster Loan Data**: Verified loss and approved loan amounts by city, county, zip code, and state. Available on Data.gov.
- **PPP Loan Data**: Full CSV download available (COVID-era program).
- **CRA (Community Reinvestment Act) Data**: Small business lending data by census tract, available from FFIEC.

### Accessibility
- **Fully public and free**. Bulk CSV downloads available.
- 7(a) and 504 data available in multi-decade files.
- Geocoded to census tract level by some providers (e.g., LA Neighborhood Data for Social Change).

### How It Signals Money Movement
- **SBA 504 loans** are specifically for real estate and fixed-asset purchases (up to $5.5M). Volume by zip code directly measures small business real estate investment.
- **SBA 7(a) loans** can fund real estate acquisitions. Geographic concentration reveals where small business capital is flowing.
- **Disaster loan volume** signals recovery investment after natural disasters.
- Rising SBA loan volume in an area indicates commercial real estate demand from small businesses.
- Declining volume may signal credit tightening or economic weakness.

### Who Uses It
- Community development financial institutions (CDFIs)
- Economic development agencies
- Small business advocacy organizations
- Academic researchers
- Commercial real estate brokers (especially in small business corridors)

## 7. OPPORTUNITY ZONE / QOF INVESTMENT

### Data Sources
- **IRS Form 8996**: Filed by Qualified Opportunity Funds (QOFs), includes investment amounts and deployment locations. Self-certification mechanism.
- **IRS Form 8997**: Filed by investors in QOFs, indicating deferred capital gains amounts.
- **Treasury Working Paper (WP-123)**: Detailed analysis of QOF investment patterns from tax return data. Key finding: 68% of QOF investment goes to real estate; 95-96% goes to urban OZs.
- **CDFI Fund (cdfifund.gov)**: Lists of designated QOZ census tracts and shapefiles.
- **HUD OZ Mapping Tools**: Geographic visualization of opportunity zones.
- **NCSHA Estimates**: As of Oct 2020, 218 QOFs raised $46.9B.

### Accessibility
- **Census tract designations and shapefiles**: Free and public via CDFI Fund
- **IRS aggregate data**: Limited public access. Treasury working papers provide some aggregates.
- **Individual Form 8996/8997 data**: Private tax information
- **OZ 2.0 (enacted July 2025)**: Requires Treasury to publish annual summary reports, plus comprehensive reports in 6th and 11th years. This will SIGNIFICANTLY improve data availability.

### How It Signals Money Movement
- QOF formation and capital deployment directly measure capital gains being redirected into specific census tracts
- Geographic distribution shows which distressed areas are attracting investment capital
- Sector allocation (68% real estate) quantifies how much OZ capital targets property
- Urban concentration (95-96%) reveals that most OZ capital bypasses rural areas
- OZ 2.0 enhanced reporting requirements will create a new, ongoing public data stream

### Who Uses It
- Impact investors and ESG-focused funds
- Community development organizations
- Municipal economic development offices
- Real estate developers targeting OZ census tracts
- Policy researchers evaluating OZ effectiveness

## 8. REAL ESTATE CROWDFUNDING FLOWS

### Data Sources
- **Platform-level data**: Fundrise, CrowdStreet, RealtyMogul, EquityMultiple, First National Realty Partners each publish some performance and deployment data.
- **SEC Reg D/Reg A filings (EDGAR)**: Crowdfunding offerings must file with the SEC. Searchable on EDGAR.
- **SEC Reg CF (Regulation Crowdfunding) filings**: For smaller offerings, filed with SEC.
- **Platform investor dashboards**: Track portfolio performance, distributions, property-level returns.

### Accessibility
- **SEC filings**: Free and public (EDGAR). Can track offering amounts, fund sizes, and geographic focus.
- **Platform data**: Available to registered investors on each platform. Aggregate data not centrally published.
- **Industry reports**: Published by various PropTech analysts (some free, some paid).

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Crowdfunding platform capital raises reveal retail investor appetite for real estate by geography and sector
- SEC filing data shows where new real estate offerings are being launched and at what scale
- Secondary market activity on platforms signals liquidity demand and investor sentiment
- Distribution yields and occupancy data from platform reports signal property-level performance
- Growing industry signals democratization of real estate capital beyond institutional channels

### Who Uses It
- Retail investors for portfolio allocation
- Platform operators for competitive intelligence
- Real estate developers seeking alternative capital
- SEC for regulatory oversight
- PropTech analysts tracking industry growth

## 9. iBUYER MARKET ACTIVITY

### Data Sources
- **Opendoor (OPEN) SEC filings and earnings**: Q3 2025: $915M revenue, 7.2% gross margin. Controls ~67% of iBuyer market. Cash Plus model now 35% of transaction volume.
- **Offerpad (OPAD) SEC filings and earnings**: Q3 2025: $132.7M revenue, 367 homes sold, 7% gross margin.
- **Mike DelPrete (mikedp.com)**: Independent real estate tech strategist tracking iBuyer market share, pricing, and activity by metro.
- **ATTOM Data / CoreLogic**: Track iBuyer purchase and sale activity at property level via deed records.
- **Parcl Labs / other proptech**: Some track institutional buyer activity including iBuyers by market.

### Accessibility
- **SEC filings**: Free (public companies)
- **Mike DelPrete research**: Some free, premium subscription for detailed data
- **ATTOM/CoreLogic**: Paid data subscriptions
- **Individual property records**: Public (county recorder)

### How It Signals Money Movement
- iBuyer acquisition volume is a DIRECT measure of algorithmic/institutional capital entering specific neighborhoods
- Geographic expansion or contraction of iBuyer operations signals market confidence
- Inventory levels (Opendoor halved inventory YoY) signal risk appetite
- Cash Plus model shift shows evolution from balance-sheet deployment to fee-based facilitation
- iBuyer pricing algorithms may influence local comps and market pricing
- "Lock-in effect" thawing in 2026 (with Fed rate cuts) is expected to increase iBuyer transaction volume

### Who Uses It
- Competing iBuyers and proptech companies
- Real estate agents monitoring market competition
- Academic researchers studying algorithmic pricing effects
- Short sellers and equity analysts
- Homeowners comparing offers

## 12. INSURANCE CLAIMS DATA

### Data Sources
- **NAIC (National Association of Insurance Commissioners)**: State-level premium and claims data. Reports that insurers paid more in claims than premiums in 18 states in 2023.
- **Swiss Re Sigma**: Global insured loss data. Estimates 1-in-10 probability of $300B+ insured loss year.
- **FEMA National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)**: Flood claims data by geography.
- **Insurance Information Institute (III)**: Catastrophe data and insurance market reports.
- **NBER Working Papers**: Academic research linking insurance pricing to home values. Key finding: homes in riskiest 10% of ZIP codes declined $43,900 (2018-2024) due to reinsurance repricing.
- **State insurance commission filings**: Premium rate change requests by insurer and geography.
- **Realtor.com surveys**: 33.7% of prospective buyers changed search geography due to insurance challenges.

### Accessibility
- **NAIC data**: Some public, detailed data requires paid subscription
- **FEMA/NFIP**: Public claims data available
- **Swiss Re Sigma**: Free published reports
- **State commission filings**: Public (varies by state)
- **Academic papers**: Free (NBER working papers)

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Rising premiums in specific markets signal CAPITAL FLIGHT risk (investors avoiding high-insurance-cost areas)
- Insurer withdrawals from markets (Florida, California, Louisiana) signal systemic risk repricing
- "Last resort" plan enrollment (doubled 2018-2023 in FL, CA, LA) marks areas where private insurance capital has left
- Claims-to-premium ratios by state reveal where property risk is mispriced
- Home value declines correlated with insurance repricing ($43.9K in riskiest 10% of ZIPs) show insurance as a price transmission mechanism
- 30% of disaster losses uninsured signals areas of hidden financial exposure
- Average premiums up 30% nationally (2020-2023); FL up 47%, with Miami (+322%), Jacksonville (+226%), Tampa (+213%) showing extreme repricing

### Who Uses It
- Insurance companies (underwriting, risk modeling)
- Real estate investors evaluating total cost of ownership
- Climate risk analysts
- Municipal bond analysts (property tax base risk)
- Federal/state regulators
- Home buyers relocating based on insurance costs

## 13. PROPERTY TAX LIEN INVESTMENT

### Data Sources
- **County tax collector/treasurer offices**: Each of the 30 states + DC allowing tax lien sales publishes auction data.
- **National Tax Lien Association (NTLA)**: Industry data. Members purchase ~80% of tax lien certificates.
- **Tax Sale Resources (taxsaleresources.com)**: Aggregated data and trend analysis. Key data: tax lien sales jumped from $3.8B (2021) to $5.02B (2024). Parcels: 1.32M (2021) to 1.52M (2024).
- **Online auction platforms**: GovEase, Bid4Assets, RealAuction publish some transaction data.

### Accessibility
- **County-level auction data**: Public (varies by county; some online, some in-person only)
- **Tax Sale Resources**: Subscription for comprehensive data
- **NTLA**: Member reports
- **Individual lien records**: Public records

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Tax lien volume signals financial distress in specific neighborhoods (property owners unable to pay taxes)
- Institutional entry (hedge funds, pension funds in 2010s) signals yield-seeking capital flowing into distressed markets
- Winning bid rates (currently 6-9%, down from 24-36% historically) signal institutional competition intensity
- Geographic distribution of delinquent parcels reveals economic weakness at hyperlocal level
- Rising volume ($3.8B to $5.02B in 3 years) reflects both distress AND growing investor demand
- 2022 spike was backlog-driven; 2024 growth is genuine market expansion

### Who Uses It
- Tax lien investors (individual and institutional)
- Distressed property investors
- Municipal finance analysts
- Community development organizations
- Title companies (lien clearance)

## 14. HARD MONEY / PRIVATE LENDING VOLUME

### Data Sources
- **Forecasa (third-party data aggregator)**: Tracks total private loan volume. 2024: 276,909 loans (up 14% YoY from 241,701 in 2023).
- **ATTOM Data Solutions**: Private financing now accounts for ~40% of all non-owner-occupied purchase volume in major metros.
- **American Association of Private Lenders (AAPLONLINE)**: Industry reports and trend data.
- **Private Lender Link (privatelenderlink.com)**: Quarterly industry updates with volume breakdowns by loan type, state, and rate.
- **Scotsman Guide**: Hard money lending industry reporting.
- **Morningstar DBRS**: Rates RTL (residential transitional loan) securitizations. 2024 saw first rated RTL securitization.

### Accessibility
- **Forecasa**: Paid data subscription
- **ATTOM**: Paid data subscription
- **AAPL reports**: Some free, some member-only
- **Private Lender Link**: Free quarterly updates
- **Securitization data**: Bloomberg/Morningstar (paid)
- **County deed records**: Public (shows private lender mortgages)

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Private lending volume is a DIRECT measure of speculative/investment capital flowing into fix-and-flip and rental property
- DSCR loans overtaking bridge loans (now >53% of volume) signals shift from speculation to long-term rental investment
- State-level rankings (CA, FL, TX top for bridge; TX, PA, FL top for DSCR) show geographic capital flow
- Securitization volume ($3.5B full-year 2023 vs. $2.25B in Q1 2024 alone) signals Wall Street appetite for residential investment exposure
- Average rates (8.5-11.2%) and LTV caps (70-75% ARV) signal market risk appetite
- 94% YoY increase in DSCR originations (tracked 42 originators) signals massive capital rotation into rental property

### Who Uses It
- Private lenders and mortgage brokers
- Fix-and-flip investors
- Rental property investors
- Wall Street securitization desks
- Real estate market analysts
- Regulators monitoring shadow banking

## 15. TITLE INSURANCE VOLUME

### Accessibility
- **ALTA reports**: Free published summaries
- **State regulatory data**: Public (varies by state)

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Title insurance volume is a near real-time PROXY for real estate transaction volume -- virtually every purchase, sale, or refinance requires title insurance
- Premium volume up 13.2% in H1 2025 signals transaction recovery
- Title order counts are a LEADING indicator (orders placed before closings)
- Refinance vs. purchase split reveals whether capital is entering market (purchases) vs. being extracted (cash-out refis) vs. being restructured (rate-and-term refis)
- Geographic breakdown of orders shows which markets are most active
- 6% annual increase in residential property transactions directly correlated with title insurance demand

### Who Uses It
- Title companies for market positioning
- Mortgage lenders for volume forecasting
- Real estate brokers for market timing
- Housing economists
- Real estate investors monitoring market velocity

## 17. MOVING COMPANY / MIGRATION DATA

### Data Sources
- **United Van Lines National Movers Study**: Annual state-by-state migration tracking since 1977. Ranks states by inbound/outbound percentages. 2025: Oregon #1 inbound for first time.
- **U.S. Census Bureau Current Population Survey**: Annual migration data. Finding: only ~9% of Americans move annually (near all-time low), down from 20% in 1980s.
- **U-Haul Migration Trends**: Annual one-way truck rental data by market.
- **Zillow/Redfin migration analyses**: Cross-reference moving data with housing affordability and search patterns.
- **CNBC/Moovsoon**: Moving industry data aggregation.

### Accessibility
- **United Van Lines study**: Free annual report
- **Census CPS migration data**: Free and public
- **U-Haul data**: Free annual report
- **Zillow/Redfin**: Free published analyses

### How It Signals Money Movement
- Net migration directly predicts housing demand in destination markets and declining demand in origin markets
- Affordability-driven migration (42% of moves are housing-related per Census) signals capital reallocation from expensive to affordable markets
- Outbound patterns from CA, NY, IL, MA signal capital exodus from high-cost states
- Inbound patterns to Sun Belt, South Atlantic states signal capital inflow
- Moving volume for a market is a LEADING indicator for home sales, rental demand, and commercial RE demand
- Short-distance moves (within metro) now dominate, suggesting people staying in broader market but seeking affordability at edges

### Who Uses It
- Real estate developers for site selection
- Retailers for store location planning
- Economic development agencies
- Municipal planners
- Residential brokers and builders
- CRE investors tracking population-driven demand

## 18. USPS CHANGE OF ADDRESS DATA

### Data Sources
- **USPS Population Mobility Trends (PMT)**: Aggregated COA data combined with 2020 Census demographics, showing top 9 destination ZIPs for each source ZIP. Available at postalpro.usps.com/pmt.
- **HUD/USPS Vacancy Data (huduser.gov/portal/datasets/usps.html)**: Quarterly aggregate data on "Vacant" and "No-Stat" addresses nationwide. Universe of all U.S. addresses, updated quarterly. Partnership since 2005.
- **Regrid (regrid.com/vacancy)**: USPS vacancy data at the PARCEL level nationwide.
- **Snowflake Data Marketplace**: USPS data documentation and access.

### Accessibility
- **PMT data**: Available through USPS (unclear if free or paid; marketed to multiple industries)
- **HUD vacancy data**: Free and public download
- **Regrid parcel-level data**: Paid subscription
- **NOTE**: USPS discontinued its public COA data product in June 2023. PMT is the current replacement.

### How It Signals Money Movement
- COA data at ZIP level directly measures population movement, a precursor to housing demand shifts
- Vacancy data distinguishes growth areas (new construction = more No-Stat addresses) from declining areas (abandonment = more Vacant addresses)
- Quarterly updates provide near-real-time migration signals
- TX gained 85K+ net residents, CA lost 239K+ net residents (annualized) -- enormous capital flow implications
- Parcel-level vacancy data (Regrid) enables neighborhood-level investment targeting
- Increasing AMS address counts with rising No-Stat addresses signals new construction/development activity

### Who Uses It
- USPS markets to real estate, construction, retail, and marketing industries
- HUD researchers and community development practitioners
- Academic researchers studying neighborhood change
- PropTech companies building migration analytics
- Direct mail marketers (original use case)

## 19. UTILITY CONNECTION / NEW SERVICE REQUESTS

### Data Sources
- **Local utility companies**: New service connection requests are tracked internally but rarely published systematically.
- **Building permit data (Census Bureau)**: Housing starts and building permits are closely correlated with utility connections. Monthly Census reports available.
- **State-level infrastructure programs**: Washington State's CHIP program tracking reveals utility connection demand ($48M in requests for $12.7M in funding in 2024).
- **Conference Board Leading Economic Index**: Includes housing starts (which require utility connections) as a component.

### Accessibility
- **Utility company data**: Generally NOT publicly available. Would require FOIA or data partnerships with specific utilities.
- **Building permits**: Free and public (Census Bureau, monthly).
- **State infrastructure program data**: Public (varies by state).

### How It Signals Money Movement
- New utility connections are a LEADING indicator of real estate development -- you connect utilities BEFORE occupancy
- Electric/gas/water new connection counts directly measure new housing and commercial space coming online
- Disconnection data signals vacancy/abandonment
- Utility load growth forecasts (used for infrastructure planning) incorporate expected development
- This is an UNDEREXPLOITED signal -- utility companies have hyperlocal data that is rarely used for real estate analysis
- Could potentially be obtained via FOIA requests to municipal utilities or public utility commission filings

### Who Uses It
- Utility companies for infrastructure planning
- Municipal planners
- Home builders (monitoring competitive activity)
- Very few real estate investors currently use this data systematically

## 20. SCHOOL ENROLLMENT DATA

### Data Sources
- **National Center for Education Statistics (NCES)**: K-12 enrollment data by school district. Annual data with ~2 year lag.
- **State education departments**: More current enrollment data at school/district level.
- **Common Core of Data (CCD)**: NCES database of public school enrollment.
- **Individual school district reports**: Often published monthly or annually.
- **Higher education enrollment**: IPEDS (Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System) via NCES.
- **Yardi Matrix**: Student housing market reports tracking university enrollment and housing demand.
- **Cushman & Wakefield**: Student housing trends and valuation indices.

### Accessibility
- **NCES/CCD**: Free and public (nces.ed.gov). Lag of 1-2 years.
- **State education data**: Generally free and public (varies by state).
- **School district data**: Public (varies; some publish detailed data, others require FOIA).
- **Yardi Matrix/Cushman**: Paid subscriptions.

### How It Signals Money Movement
- K-12 enrollment growth in a school district signals FAMILY migration into that area, a strong demand signal for family-sized housing
- Enrollment decline signals population loss and potential housing demand weakness
- School quality ratings drive housing premiums (well-documented in real estate economics)
- University enrollment changes directly drive student housing demand in college towns
- International student enrollment decline (11%+, 130K fewer students in 2025-2026) signals reduced demand in university markets
- Hispanic student growth (projected +1M through 2031) signals long-term demand in specific regions
- This is a LAGGING indicator (families enroll after moving) but useful for confirming trend direction

### Who Uses It
- Residential real estate agents (school quality as selling point)
- Family-housing developers
- Student housing investors (REITs, developers)
- Municipal planners and school boards
- Demographers and population researchers
- Real estate economists studying price premiums

## KEY FINDINGS & UNDEREXPLOITED SIGNALS

### Most Accessible & Underused
1. **Census Bureau Business Formation Statistics** -- Free, monthly, state-level, NAICS-coded. Real estate LLC formations are a leading indicator that almost nobody uses for market analysis.
2. **HUD/USPS Vacancy Data** -- Free, quarterly, census-tract level. Universe of all U.S. addresses. Underused outside academic research.
3. **SBA 504 Loan Data** -- Free, geocoded. Directly measures small business real estate investment by zip code.
4. **SEC Form D Filings** -- Free. Tracks every private real estate fund raising capital, with fund size, investor count, and geographic data.

### Most Valuable But Hardest to Access
1. **Utility new connection data** -- Hyperlocal leading indicator. Locked inside utility companies.
2. **1031 Exchange volume by market** -- Would be the single best measure of investor capital recycling. IRS does not publish this.
3. **Private lending data at property level** -- Forecasa and ATTOM have it, but it is expensive.

### Emerging Data Streams
1. **OZ 2.0 mandatory reporting** (enacted July 2025) will create a new annual public data stream on opportunity zone capital flows by census tract.
2. **iBuyer transaction data** is becoming increasingly trackable via public company filings and property records.
3. **Insurance repricing data** is becoming a powerful NEW real estate signal as climate risk reshapes markets (FL, CA, LA most affected).
